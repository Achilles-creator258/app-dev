## Kingdom of Heaven (2005) – Movie Review

### A. Introduction
*Kingdom of Heaven* is a 2005 historical drama directed by Ridley Scott. Set in the 12th century during the Crusades, it tells the story of Balian, a French blacksmith who unexpectedly inherits his father’s title and travels to Jerusalem. 

Beyond its epic scale, the movie digs into deeper themes: faith, personal honor, leadership, and the complex struggle for peaceful coexistence between Christians and Muslims during a time of intense religious conflict.

- **Genre:** Historical Drama, Action, Adventure, War
- **Release Year:** 2005
- **Director:** Ridley Scott

---

### B. Synopsis
After losing his wife and child, Balian’s life takes a dramatic turn when a knight named Godfrey reveals he is Balian’s father and invites him to join the crusade in Jerusalem. Facing tragedy back home, Balian accepts the calling and sets off for the Holy Land.

Upon arriving in Jerusalem, Balian gets swept into a web of political tension and religious rivalry. He earns the respect of King Baldwin IV, a wise young king living with leprosy who works tirelessly to keep a fragile peace with the Muslim forces. But peace is constantly threatened by hot-headed noblemen eager to push the kingdom into total war.

When conflict becomes unavoidable, Balian steps up to lead the defense of Jerusalem against Sultan Saladin’s massive army. Through the struggle, Balian learns that real leadership isn't about chasing power or glory—it’s about protecting the vulnerable and holding on to your integrity when everything else falls apart.

---

### C. Key Characters

- **Balian of Ibelin** (*Orlando Bloom*) – A grieving blacksmith who grows into a noble knight tasked with defending Jerusalem.
- **Godfrey of Ibelin** (*Liam Neeson*) – Balian’s father, a respected warrior who passes down his values of honor and duty.
- **King Baldwin IV** (*Edward Norton*) – The leprosy-stricken King of Jerusalem whose calm wisdom holds the kingdom together.
- **Sibylla** (*Eva Green*) – Princess of Jerusalem and sister to Baldwin IV, caught between love, duty, and survival.
- **Saladin** (*Ghassan Massoud*) – The Muslim Sultan and commander; portrayed with immense dignity, discipline, and strategic brilliance.
- **Guy de Lusignan** (*Marton Csokas*) – An arrogant nobleman eager to break the peace treaty and drag both sides into war.
- **Tiberias** (*Jeremy Irons*) – King Baldwin’s pragmatic military advisor who fights to keep order.

---

### D. Personal Review
I picked up this movie because I’ve always had a soft spot for medieval history—knights, sieges, and grand kingdoms. What stood out to me about *Kingdom of Heaven* is that it doesn't just rely on massive battle sequences; it takes time to explore meaningful ideas about leadership and respect between different cultures and faiths.

> "Religion should not be used as an excuse to hate or hurt others. Respect and peace are far more important than fighting just because people hold different beliefs."

#### Three Standout Aspects

1. **The Siege Warfare & Atmosphere:** The battle scenes feel incredibly grounded and intense. The scale of the armies, medieval tactics, and siege equipment make the action feel authentic rather than standard Hollywood spectacle.
2. **Memorable Leadership:** The dynamic between Balian and King Baldwin IV is fantastic. It highlights that strong leadership stems from tough moral choices and protecting people, not just flexing authority.
3. **The Core Message:** The film offers a thoughtful perspective on religion. It shows how faith shouldn't be weaponized to justify cruelty, making a strong case for tolerance and mutual respect over fanaticism.

**Personal Rating:** **9/10**

If you enjoy historical epics like *Gladiator* or *Braveheart*, this is definitely worth your time. It’s a long watch, but the compelling characters, stunning visuals, and timeless themes make every minute count.
